# Cohort review, November 2026

Date: 2026-11-20. Reviewed: `main` at `0e4239c` (Stages 0 to 4, plus the maps
and polish of PR #12).

How it was reviewed: about 40 scripted battles through the web app's own
server code on all four maps, 50 typed orders through the order reader, a
phone-sized browser session of a two-player battle, and a live battle with
the AI connected. Every finding below was seen happen; none is a guess from
reading code.

**Status, 2026-11-21.** Zach answered the questions at the end (rulings in
`docs/DECISIONS.md`). The bug sweep is done on the `bug-sweep` branch: B1 to
B5, L1, L6, L7, L8, H1, H2 and H3, plus one bug found on the way (two units
making for each other in a wood could sidestep past each other for ever).
One more hole was found and is listed as L12. Next: the battle's pace (L2,
L9).

**The short version.** The app runs end to end and nothing crashes: muster,
orders, turns, fog of war, the verdict and the replay all work, alone and by
link. The engine underneath is sound and well tested. What holds it back as
a game is that battles are over in four or five turns before a plan can
matter, the computer opponent only knows how to charge, your own units
charge too unless told otherwise, and archers cannot shoot. There are eight
real bugs. The AI was not working at all (its model had been retired); that
is fixed in the same change as this review.

Sizes: **S** is an hour or two, **M** half a day to a day, **L** several days.

---

## 1. What works

- **The whole loop.** Muster a battle on any of four maps, alone or by link,
  give orders, read the dispatch, repeat to a verdict, replay it.
- **Fog of war held on the server.** A side's browser is never sent an
  enemy it has not seen.
- **Saved battles** survive a restart. A battle left for days is still
  there.
- **The map** reads well as an old battle map on a phone and a desktop.
- **Plain orders** ("archers to D7-NE", "everyone advance", "spears defend
  the bridge") are read instantly and correctly, and the read-back says what
  will happen before anything is sent.
- **Ambush, hiding and searching** work as ruled: a hidden unit lets a
  careful column pass, catches a marching one, and is found by a search.
- **Tap a unit, tap a place** writes a correct order on a phone.
- **Tests:** 441 pass; 480 games with silent commanders all end.

## 2. Bugs

Ranked by how much they hurt play.

| # | Bug | What I saw | Fix | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **A careful or crowded unit can never enter woods, marsh or a hill.** | "Spears march carefully to D5-NE" on Thornwood: the spears stopped at the treeline for ever. Three units stacked on a road tile in front of a wood never moved again, which froze the computer's whole army for 50 minutes of battle. Cause: a careful march has 1.5 movement, a wood costs 2. | A unit that has not moved this tick may always enter one tile, whatever it costs. | S |
| B2 | **Routed units still cut down fleeing enemies.** | "Enemy spears broke and fled", then in the same breath "enemy spears cut down 7 of the fleeing Horse". | Broken units do not pursue. | S |
| B3 | **"Spring the trap" orders the whole army to charge.** | With one unit in ambush and the horse held in reserve, "spring the trap" sent the horse in too. | It applies only to units lying in ambush; with none, say so. | S |
| B4 | **One unclear order throws away all the others.** | Four orders, one with an unclear place: nothing was sent, and on the next send every unit followed the standing orders and charged. | Send the clear orders; show the unclear one as still to answer. Let the player choose "send the rest". | M |
| B5 | **Useless questions in big woods.** | On Thornwood: "Which woods: the woods 100 m east, or the woods 125 m east?" Both are the same forest, and the two buttons are bare grid squares. | When the candidates are parts of one feature or nearly the same distance, take the nearest. Label choices by direction and distance. | S |
| B6 | **Zero-distance moves are reported.** | "Enemy horse moved 0 m east (stopped by Horse)", every turn of a fight. | Fixed in this change: a unit that got nowhere is not said to have moved. | done |
| B7 | **Unit labels pile on top of each other on the map.** | Four units on neighbouring tiles: the names overlap into an unreadable smear; the same for "last seen" labels. | Label once per cluster, or show names only for the picked unit and on zoom. | M |
| B8 | **Tap targets on the map are 21 pixels.** | On a phone the counters are about half the size a finger needs; stacked units cannot be picked apart. | Bigger hit areas; tapping a stack lists its units to pick from. | M |

Smaller: the tent's title wraps to two lines on a phone and pushes the map
down; the hint box permanently covers the bottom of the map; the dispatch
said "Swords's ranks" (fixed in this change).

## 3. Logic holes

Things that work as built but make the game worse.

| # | Hole | What I saw | Proposal | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Your own units charge unless told not to.** | Both scenarios give both sides the standing order "advance". A unit you give no order marches at the nearest enemy. Give three units orders and forget the fourth, and it attacks alone. | For a human side, the default is hold. Standing advance stays for the computer. (The hotseat's `--auto` play keeps its rule.) | S |
| L2 | **Battles are over before a plan can matter.** | Typical battle: contact on turn 2, half the units broken by turn 4, verdict by turn 5, 60 to 80 minutes of battle time. A unit in three fights takes three rounds of losses and morale damage in one ten-minute tick; the archers broke in a single round. Morale never comes back. | Three changes, tuned together with `npm run soak`: (a) a unit fighting several enemies splits its effort, so it does not take full damage from each; (b) slower morale loss per round; (c) a broken unit out of contact can rally once. Target: 10 to 15 turns. | L |
| L3 | **The computer opponent only charges.** | It marches every unit at your nearest unit, by the same road, onto the same tile (291 men on one tile, "critical" crowding, twice). It never hides, holds ground, guards a crossing, uses objectives or retreats. | A simple planner per battle: pick a posture from the ground (hold a crossing, take the hill, advance in line), keep units in line abreast, pull back a wavering unit. Deterministic and seeded, no AI model needed. | L |
| L4 | **Archers cannot shoot.** | Archers stood two tiles from a fight for five turns and did nothing; in contact they lose badly to everything. "Archers shoot" is the most natural order a player types and the game has no answer. | Ranged fire: units with a `range` weapon shoot the nearest visible enemy in range each tick; light losses, morale pressure, less in woods and when hidden in marsh. | L |
| L5 | **Everyone takes the same path.** | Units ordered forward converge on the road and stack on one tile, taking crowding penalties the player never chose. | Path-finding treats a tile already holding a friend as dearer, so a column spreads out. | M |
| L6 | **Crownhill's battle uses a tenth of the map.** | Both armies start 225 m apart in the north-west corner. The bridge, the fords and the south never matter. | Deploy Crownhill's scenarios like the new maps: either side of the river, a real approach march. | S |
| L7 | **Mutual surprise.** | Two columns meeting in a wood are both "caught on the march" and both get the bonus. | Surprise only when one side saw the other first. | S |
| L8 | **The first turn can run three ticks with no say.** | An ambush was laid and sprung inside turn 1 (30 minutes) because nothing stopped the clock. | Stop the turn when a unit finishes hiding or reaches its objective. | S |
| L9 | **Winning with your army in flight.** | A "Victory" declared the instant the last three enemy units broke in the same tick, with one of my own units fled and another in flight. Both armies still had about half their men. | The verdict needs a side still able to hold the field; otherwise a draw or "bloody stalemate". | M |
| L10 | **No way to target an enemy unit.** | "Charge the enemy archers" can only mean "attack the square they were seen in". | An attack order may name a seen enemy unit; the unit follows it while it stays in sight. | M |
| L12 | **"Advance" knows where unseen enemies are.** | A unit told to advance, and the computer's whole army, march straight at the nearest enemy whether or not anyone has seen it. Only hidden units are spared. | Advance on the nearest enemy your side has seen or last saw; with none, toward the enemy's edge of the field. To be done with the thinking opponent (L3). | M |
| L11 | **Withdraw is only two tiles.** | "Everyone fall back" moved units two tiles to the woods at the map edge and stopped; the enemy caught them next turn. | Fine as a rule, but say where they will stop in the read-back. | S |

## 5. Making it a better game

Beyond fixes. In the order I would do them.

1. **Longer, slower battles (L2) and a thinking opponent (L3).** Together
   these are most of the gap between "it runs" and "it is a game". Nothing
   else matters much until a battle lasts long enough to have a plan and the
   enemy has one too.
2. **Ranged fire (L4).** Archers are a quarter of every army and currently a
   liability.
3. **Objectives in every scenario.** They exist and work, but only if the
   player types one in at the muster. Each map should come with its own
   ("hold the Old Mill Bridge"), so there is a reason to cross the field.
4. **A reason to keep your men alive:** carry an army from one battle to the
   next (the veterans engine exists and is tested; nothing shows it yet).
   Captains with names, who are wounded, captured or killed, and remembered.
5. **Commanders with a personality** (ruled October 14: equal quality,
   different character), starting with the enemy's: a cautious one, a rash
   one. The planner of L3 is where this plugs in.
6. **The 2 km map** and scenarios bigger than four units a side, once
   battles are slower.
7. **Hosting and a "your move" message,** when friends are to play.

## 6. The AI

Done in the same change as this review; details in `docs/ai/MODELS.md`.

- **It was not working.** The default model (`llama-3.1-8b-instant`) is no
  longer offered by Groq, so every call failed and the game fell back to the
  grammar and plain reports without saying so.
- **Model:** `openai/gpt-oss-20b`, the cheapest Groq offers and the fastest
  of the three tested. On the benchmark's orders it did as well as the
  models that cost twice and nine times as much.
- **Grammar first, AI second.** A plain order never costs an AI call. Only
  lines the grammar cannot read, or lines that say two things, go to the
  model. In the benchmark a quarter of the orders needed no AI at all; in
  real play, where most orders come from tapping the map, far more. With
  the tuned prompt the model read all 42 benchmark orders.
- **A new order prompt** with unit ids, the map's named places, seen enemies
  by grid square, and a strict schema the provider enforces.
- **The reader understands more:** several units in one order, compass
  directions and distances ("200 m north"), "everyone else", an enemy unit
  as a target, and notes that do not block the turn ("I recorded the first
  step; order the second next turn").
- **Dispatches are worked out in code first.** A turn's fighting is boiled
  down to one line per unit before the model sees it. The plain report went
  from twelve lines to two, and the narrator can no longer muddle who lost
  what.
- **Cost:** about $0.0002 a player turn with the AI on, so roughly 20 cents
  per thousand turns. Your Groq account is on the free tier, which costs
  nothing but allows about three AI turns a minute across all players.
